# Requirements from the pinned text

These sentences were read from the pinned sources on 2026-10-06. Diátaxis has no MUST or SHALL keywords, so these are its key principles, quoted as written (some are the bold principle statements, quoted without the surrounding sentence). Apply the ones that match the kind of document. Each is labelled with the page and heading it comes from.

## Tutorials

Source: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/evildmp/diataxis-documentation-framework/957c09ca40b4a1edc23874f713e01937d50d54d5/source/tutorials.rst

- **Tutorials § Deliver visible results early and often.** Every step the learner follows should produce a comprehensible result, however small.
- **Tutorials § Ruthlessly minimise explanation.** Instead, provide a link or reference to that explanation, so that it's available, but doesn't get in the way.
- **Tutorials § Ignore options and alternatives.** Your job is to guide the learner to a successful conclusion.
- **Tutorials § Aspire to perfect reliability.** A tutorial must inspire confidence.
- **Tutorials § Aspire to perfect reliability.** At every stage, when you ask your student to do something, they must see the result you promise.

## How-to guides

Source: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/evildmp/diataxis-documentation-framework/957c09ca40b4a1edc23874f713e01937d50d54d5/source/how-to-guides.rst

- **How-to guides § How-to guides addressed to problems.** How-to guides must be written from the perspective of the user, not of the machinery.
- **How-to guides § What how-to guides are not.** How-to guides are wholly distinct from tutorials
- **How-to guides § Address real-world complexity.** You can't address every possible case, so you must find ways to remain open to the range of possibilities, in such a way that the user can adapt your guidance to their needs.
- **How-to guides § Omit the unnecessary.** It should start and end in some reasonable, meaningful place, and require the reader to join it up to their own work.
- **How-to guides § Provide a set of instructions.** A how-to guide should address how the user thinks as well as what the user does.
- **How-to guides § Pay attention to naming.** Choose titles that say exactly what a how-to guide shows.

## Reference

Source: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/evildmp/diataxis-documentation-framework/957c09ca40b4a1edc23874f713e01937d50d54d5/source/reference.rst

- **Reference § Reference as description.** There should be no doubt or ambiguity in reference; it should be wholly authoritative.
- **Reference § Describe and only describe.** Instead, link to how-to guides, explanation and introductory tutorials.
- **Reference § Adopt standard patterns.** Reference material is useful when it is consistent.
- **Reference § Respect the structure of the machinery.** the structure of the documentation should mirror the structure of the product

## Explanation

Source: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/evildmp/diataxis-documentation-framework/957c09ca40b4a1edc23874f713e01937d50d54d5/source/explanation.rst

- **Explanation § Provide context.** Provide background and context in your explanation
- **Explanation § Talk about the subject.** you should be able to place an implicit (or even explicit) _about_ in front of each title.
- **Explanation § Admit opinion and perspective.** Explanation can and must consider alternatives
- **Explanation § Keep explanation closely bounded.** One risk of explanation is that it tends to absorb other things.

## The compass

Source: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/evildmp/diataxis-documentation-framework/957c09ca40b4a1edc23874f713e01937d50d54d5/source/compass.rst

- **The compass.** To use the compass, just two questions need to be asked: _action or cognition?_ _acquisition or application?_
- **Using the compass.** The compass forces you to stop and reconsider.
