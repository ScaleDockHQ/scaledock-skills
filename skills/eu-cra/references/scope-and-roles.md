# Scope, roles and product classes

**Commercial activity** (recital 15) can be shown by charging a price, charging for technical support beyond cost recovery, an intention to monetise (for example a platform through which other services are monetised), requiring personal data processing as a condition of use for reasons other than security, compatibility or interoperability, or accepting donations beyond costs. Donations without a profit intention are not commercial activity. A fee charged solely to recover the costs of a service, and products a public administration develops exclusively for its own use, are not commercial activity on those grounds alone (recital 16).

**Remote data processing** is in scope when the manufacturer designs and develops the software for it, or has it developed under its responsibility, and the product cannot perform one of its functions without it (Art. 3(2)). A mobile app's backend API built by the manufacturer is in scope; websites that do not support the product's functionality, and cloud services designed outside the manufacturer's responsibility, are not (recitals 11 and 12). Recital 12 adds that Directive (EU) 2022/2555 (NIS2) applies to cloud computing services such as SaaS, PaaS and IaaS.

**Testing and fairs.** Member States may not prevent unfinished software from being made available for a limited testing period with a visible sign that it does not comply and is only for testing (Art. 4(3)); this does not apply to safety components under other harmonisation legislation (Art. 4(4)). Non-compliant products may be shown at fairs and demonstrations with a visible sign (Art. 4(2)).

**Products designed before the CRA applies.** Products placed on the market before 11 December 2027 are subject to the CRA only if substantially modified from that date, except that Art. 14 reporting applies to them all (Art. 69(2), (3)). Units placed on the market after that date are new placings: they need a risk assessment, conformity assessment, declaration and CE marking, but the guidance says they need not be redesigned where the risk assessment shows existing measures are adequate, and historical design or test records need not be recreated (guidance points 33 to 39).

## Free and open-source software

- **Definition.** Software whose source code is openly shared and made available under a licence granting all rights to make it freely accessible, usable, modifiable and redistributable (Art. 3(48)). The guidance reads "openly shared" as publicly available: code shared only with paying customers is not free and open-source software for CRA purposes (guidance point 46).
- **Only monetised supply is in scope.** Free and open-source software is in scope only when made available in the course of a commercial activity. Supply that the manufacturer does not monetise is not commercial activity; how development was financed, contributions or funding from manufacturers, and regular releases do not by themselves make it commercial; a component intended for integration is made available only if its original manufacturer monetises it; and not-for-profit organisations that use all earnings after costs for not-for-profit objectives are not engaged in commercial activity (recital 18).
- **Contributors are out of scope.** The CRA does not apply to people who contribute source code to free and open-source software that is not under their responsibility (recital 18). The guidance treats the software as under the responsibility of those who publish it and control development, releases and distribution (maintainers); commit access alone does not make someone responsible (guidance point 49).
- **Guidance examples (non-binding).** Charging for binaries places the software on the market (point 51). A free community edition is a different product from a paid edition; only the paid one is placed on the market, and a legal person publishing the community edition is its steward (points 52, 53). Optional paid consultancy around freely downloadable software does not place it on the market, but a paid version bundling support does (points 56, 57). A donation link does not, unless access, essential functions or updates are in practice reserved for donors (points 61, 62).
- **Hosting is not supply.** Hosting products on open repositories, package managers or collaboration platforms is not making them available; such services are distributors only if they themselves supply the software in the course of a commercial activity (recital 20).

## Open-source software stewards

A steward is a legal person, other than a manufacturer, that systematically provides sustained support for the development of specific free and open-source products intended for commercial activities, and ensures their viability (Art. 3(14)). Recital 19 names foundations and entities that develop and publish such software in a business context, and lists hosting and managing collaboration platforms, hosting code, governing or managing, and steering development as sustained support. Stewards may not affix the CE marking (recital 19).

Art. 24 applies from 11 December 2027; the Commission states that stewards' reporting under Art. 24(3) starts then, not on 11 September 2026 (Art. 71(2); Commission reporting page; FAQ 5.5). Fines do not apply to infringements by stewards (Art. 64(10)(b)). The guidance scales Art. 24(3) by the kind of support: non-technical support only means no mandatory reporting; providing infrastructure means reporting severe incidents affecting it; providing engineering resources means reporting actively exploited vulnerabilities (guidance points 79 to 82).

**Becoming a manufacturer.** An importer or distributor that places a product on the market under its own name or trademark, or substantially modifies a product already on the market, is a manufacturer under Arts. 13 and 14 (Art. 21). Anyone else who substantially modifies a product and makes it available is a manufacturer for the affected part, or for the whole product if the change affects its cybersecurity as a whole (Art. 22).

**Substantial modification** is a change after placing on the market that affects compliance with Annex I Part I or changes the intended purpose assessed (Art. 3(30)). The guidance says security updates are generally not substantial modifications when they do not change the intended purpose or introduce new risks, even with significant technical changes, but an update can be one when it materially changes data flows, dependencies or externally reachable interfaces in a way the risk assessment did not foresee (guidance points 108, 109).

All economic operators keep the names of their suppliers and customers for 10 years (Art. 23).

## Product classes

| Class               | Test                                                                                                                           | Conformity assessment                                                                                                                                      |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Default             | No core functionality from Annex III or IV. The term "default" is the guidance's, not the Regulation's (guidance footnote 22). | Any Art. 32(1) procedure, including module A.                                                                                                              |
| Important, class I  | Core functionality of an Annex III class I category (Art. 7(1)).                                                               | Module A only if harmonised standards, common specifications or a certificate at least "substantial" are applied in full; otherwise B+C or H (Art. 32(2)). |
| Important, class II | Core functionality of an Annex III class II category.                                                                          | B+C, H, or a certificate at least "substantial" (Art. 32(3)).                                                                                              |
| Critical            | Core functionality of an Annex IV category (Art. 8).                                                                           | A European cybersecurity certificate where a delegated act under Art. 8(1) requires it; otherwise the class II procedures (Art. 32(4)).                    |

**Annex III class I**: identity management and privileged access management (including authentication and access control readers, biometric readers); standalone and embedded browsers; password managers; anti-malware; VPN products; network management systems; SIEM systems; boot managers; PKI and certificate issuance software; physical and virtual network interfaces; operating systems; routers, internet modems and switches; microprocessors, microcontrollers, ASICs and FPGAs with security-related functionalities; smart home general purpose virtual assistants; smart home products with security functionalities (door locks, security cameras, baby monitors, alarms); internet-connected toys with social interactive or location tracking features; personal wearables with a health monitoring purpose outside the medical device regulations, or for children.

**Annex III class II**: hypervisors and container runtime systems; firewalls and intrusion detection and prevention systems; tamper-resistant microprocessors; tamper-resistant microcontrollers.

**Annex IV**: hardware devices with security boxes; smart meter gateways and other devices for advanced security purposes, including secure cryptoprocessing; smartcards or similar devices, including secure elements.

**Core functionality decides, not integration.** Integrating a product with an Annex III core functionality does not by itself make the host product important (Art. 7(1)). Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/2392 gives the technical description of each category (Art. 2 and Annexes I and II) and illustrates the test: a news app embedding a browser is not a browser (recital 3); extra functions do not remove a core functionality, so an operating system with a calculator is still an operating system (recital 4); SOAR software is generally not a SIEM, and a smartphone is generally not an operating system or password manager (recital 5). Its descriptions add, for example, single sign-on, federated identity and multi-factor authentication software to identity management; browsers with AI agent integration to browsers; container network interfaces and VPN interfaces to virtual network interfaces; wireless access points to switches; and payment terminals, hardware security modules, tachographs, TPMs and eUICCs to the critical categories. Tamper-resistant microprocessors and microcontrollers are designed for AVA_VAN level 2 or 3; secure elements for at least AVA_VAN.4 (Annex I class II points 3 and 4; Annex II point 3).

The classification does not change the risk assessment: every manufacturer assesses risks and applies Annex I proportionately, whatever the class (Implementing Reg. 2025/2392 recital 6).

## Interplay with the AI Act

A product that is also a high-risk AI system under Art. 6 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 is deemed to meet that Act's Art. 15 cybersecurity requirements when it meets Annex I Parts I and II and the EU declaration of conformity demonstrates it; the AI Act conformity procedure applies, except for important and critical products where the AI Act would allow internal control only (Art. 12(1) to (3)).

## Common mistakes

- Treating "free of charge" as out of scope: a free product that is monetised in another way is supplied in the course of a commercial activity (Art. 3(13); recital 15).
- Classifying by marketing claims or by an embedded component rather than by the product's core functionality (Art. 7(1); guidance point 142).
- Assuming a foundation hosting a project has no obligations: it may be a steward for the projects it sustains (Art. 3(14); recital 19).
- Assuming legacy products are untouched until 2027: Art. 14 reporting applies to them from 11 September 2026 (Art. 69(3)).
